# Certification development method

This is a working method distilled from the owner's prior certification explorations and assessment skill. Adapt it with the domain owner; do not present it as a complete transcription of their expertise or a mandatory accreditation standard. The artifact templates are [here](../assets/templates.md).

## Program intent

Start with who should earn the credential and what a reasonable third party should infer from it. Specify role scope, level of independent performance, context, exclusions, program owner and decisions. Separate education participation from demonstrated competence. Work backward from the intended claim to evidence and assessment methods. Record why a certification is useful compared with training or an attendance certificate. Establish resource/timing assumptions without buying infrastructure.

Prepare a development plan with artifacts, dependencies, responsible roles and evidence needed to advance. Include program policies and operating responsibility early enough to affect design. When information is missing, draft alternatives and instruments that help the owner decide. Do not mistake a blank record shell for a useful first deliverable.

## Job and competency analysis

Use documentation, existing role material and actual expert input to identify the work. A useful task describes an action on an object under conditions with an observable result. Capture needed knowledge/skills separately. For example, 'Configure a service account to read an approved dataset without write access' is assessable; 'Understands security' is not a task statement.

Prepare a sampling/selection rationale and disclose whose work is represented. Plan interviews/workshops to elicit typical, difficult, high-consequence and infrequent work. Develop task survey questions with defined importance, frequency and consequence anchors as appropriate. Preserve actual response counts, missingness, contradictory findings and rationale for task inclusion. Do not calculate a supposedly universal importance-times-frequency weight.

Group tasks into competencies with observable proficiency anchors. Show the task-to-competency mapping and omissions. Distinguish document-derived hypotheses, expert-reviewed statements and field-study findings. A proposed inventory is useful, but never label it a completed validated study. Use the findings to inform assessment specifications.

## Assessment design

Identify what evidence of performance the credential needs, what can reasonably be sampled, and the limits of each method. Build domains/objectives, weights with reasons, method mix, form length, timing proposal, scoring, resources and intended candidate level. Avoid using training chapter length as an automatic proxy for importance.

Distinguish task importance, cognitive demand, intended item challenge and empirical item difficulty. Bloom levels describe the mental task; asking for a longer answer does not automatically assess higher-order thinking. Practical work may need a simulation or artifact with observable criteria. Do not replace required performance with recall solely because multiple-choice is easy to generate.

Record how integer form counts approximate the blueprint and whether any important domain receives no items. Item-count weighting is the helper's supported model; points-based/multidimensional designs need a documented separate specification. Keep candidate exposure and alternate-form requirements in view without pretending that parallel blueprints make forms equivalent.

## Item development

Write the objective and source location before polishing wording. Give enough scenario information for a supported answer. For selected response, assess a real decision with plausible alternatives and explain why the alternatives fail under those conditions. Review answer cues, inconsistent option lengths, ambiguity, double negatives and incidental trivia. Preserve provenance and author/revision.

For practical tasks, specify safe setup, starting state, resources, required deliverables and observable scoring anchors. Explicitly distinguish full, partial and zero credit; avoid vague 'excellent/good/poor' labels without observable behavior. Provide calibration examples for raters separately from candidate instructions. Do not claim rater agreement without data.

Learning explanations can help item authors and reviewers. Keep them out of candidate-facing live exam material. Separate approved practice examples from live questions and disclose intended exposure. Draft item banks can be generated; acceptance requires the program's actual review process.

## Review

Give reviewers exact IDs/revisions, relevant source excerpts/locations, objectives, keys/rubrics and a structured decision request. Separate content accuracy, measurement design, language/accessibility and fairness considerations. Reviewers need not all do every function, but the owner should choose sufficient coverage. Record role and relevant expertise without manufacturing credentials.

Preserve proposed changes and disagreements. An AI critique is useful editorial input; it is not a human panel decision. Resolve contradictions with evidence and accountable decisions. A changed prompt/key/rubric or source mapping gets a new revision and fresh review. Never mark all items approved because they pass the schema validator.

## Pilot and passing standard

Prepare administration, cohort, form identity, scoring, data handling and feedback protocols. Process only actual data for real findings. Review statistics alongside content, sample limitations, scoring quality and intended use. Model-generated responses may test software behavior; they are not candidate evidence.

Define the minimally competent performance and the standard-setting procedure before interpreting panel judgments. Document panel selection/training, rounds, disagreements, policy considerations, uncertainty and the approved rule. Keep the raw panel arithmetic separate from the owner-approved passing score and any rounding/compensation rule. Do not assume 70 percent or derive validity from alpha.

## Launch

Assemble the exact reviewed form and pin content versions. Test candidate instructions, scoring, timing, accessible delivery, identity/eligibility process, support, retakes, appeals and result communications. Prepare public guidance separately from secure content. Confirm the owner-approved passing rule and operational responsibilities.

An export is an authoring artifact until imported and verified in the actual system. A scheduled pilot is not completed delivery. Preserve verified destination, version, readback and any limitations. Use existing delivery/credential tools where appropriate, and check a named provider's current contract before implementing an adapter.

## Operations

Reconcile candidate outcomes against authoritative form/scoring versions and eligibility rules. Prepare credential intents only after the required decision. Reconcile provider state before retrying unknown outcomes and retain receipts. Track appeals, accommodations, security incidents, corrections and revocations as cases with evidence, owner and dates. Do not silently overwrite adverse or superseded results.

Use pseudonymous identifiers in routine analysis and restrict identity mappings. Apply the chosen retention and exposure policies. Evaluate actual candidate experience and program outcomes, not just issuance counts or helper pass rates.

## Maintenance

Review product changes, role evolution, performance data, exposure and candidate feedback. Trace changed sources into affected competencies, items, guides and forms. Determine semantic impact before revising. Retain old releases and approval history; plan who transitions when and how existing credentials are treated. A new form needs new evidence for claims that depend on its design; do not silently claim equating.

Renewal and retirement rules should follow the credential purpose and owner's policy. Draft communication and migration plans, get consequential decisions, and use existing authorized channels. Preserve enough restricted history to explain what was awarded and on what basis.
