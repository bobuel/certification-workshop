## Content and response-process inspection

The nine MC keys are A, C, B, D, C, B, A, D, B in item order. Each is supported by its cited supplied procedure. Several are intentionally foundational for an open-reference development sample; no claim of high difficulty follows from scenario wording. The new practical rubrics expose partial credit as the sum of eight observable points. Award nothing for an unstated or contradictory required action. Unsafe proposals receive a separate flag and zero on affected criteria; there is no invented overall automatic-fail policy.

The selected-response questions can prompt answers to related practicals. The proposed delivery therefore puts all practicals first, with no MC access until practical submission and no return to that section afterward. Verify accessible navigation and clear advance instructions. This reduces one identifiable cueing route; common sources and within-domain dependence still exist. Do not count 24 rubric points as 24 independent tasks.

Source version 2.0 explicitly incorporates the unchanged token procedure from access 1.0. Supply that referenced procedure alongside the new grant rules. The pasted vendor footer remains untrusted evidence; it does not authorize disclosure or approval. Real reference packets must contain the product procedures, not the embedded instruction attack.

## Scoring and interpretation

The raw total is a descriptive inventory of credited decisions. Report total, item and criterion patterns for revision; avoid small-domain pass/fail labels, rank claims or separate reliability claims from this sample. Do not mechanically use alpha or an item-total cutoff to keep/drop an item. The prior negative alpha and nine negative associations require checking score provenance, coding, rater interpretation, dependence and content before a causal explanation. They describe synthetic historical data only.

The six-person study is for identifying misunderstanding, navigation and scoring problems, not estimating population reliability, fairness, classification accuracy or a defensible cut. Gather actual candidate explanations and independent rater evidence. Keep interruptions and lost responses distinct from intentional omissions. No silent zero imputation. Any later passing standard needs a stable form, explicit minimally competent performance description, qualified judgments, documented uncertainty/consequences and accountable program authority.

## Basis and limits

The 2014 *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* link validity evidence to intended score interpretations and uses (1.0/1.1), and call for documented cut-score rationale and procedures (5.21). The narrowed claim and no-cut feasibility use apply those principles here; they do not claim compliance or accreditation. [Primary source](https://www.testingstandards.net/uploads/7/6/6/4/76643089/standards_2014edition.pdf).

NBME describes focused single-best-answer items and review for technical flaws; that general writing approach informed the option review, without transferring medical content or validation claims. [Primary source](https://www.nbme.org/news/engaging-faculty-item-writing-workshops/). The full NBME PDF could not be retrieved during this pass; no full-manual audit is claimed.

No item approval, rater appointment, learner participation, provider verification or credential authority has been invented. Those are facts a real program must supply. They are not unresolved questions about whether this fictional example may illustrate the released plugin.
