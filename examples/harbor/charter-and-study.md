# Harbor program development — synthetic worked example

## Charter

Purpose: demonstrate a complete from-scratch certification workflow for implementation administrators. The proposed claim is competence in the defined ingestion, access and recovery tasks of fictional Harbor v1. It excludes platform architecture, incident command, production deployment and broad data-engineering competence. No credential is offered by this example.

The hypothetical owner is the Harbor program lead. Proposed audience: administrators who operate within an approved implementation scope. The real program would need decisions on eligibility, independent performance level, operational ownership, costs, accommodations, retakes, appeals and renewal before launch. No arbitrary passing percentage is assumed.

Available evidence: three synthetic product guides. Missing real evidence: practitioner participation, job-analysis findings, item reviews, candidate performance, standard-setting judgments, and delivery verification. The simulated records in program.json rehearse these transitions; they do not resolve these real-world gaps.

## Proposed task and competency model

| Task | Observable work | Enabling knowledge/skill | Competency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Import a dataset and reconcile accepted/rejected rows before publication | CSV configuration; required fields; reconciliation; error diagnosis | C1: independently produce an evidence-supported publication decision |
| T2 | Apply dataset-specific read access and invalidate a disclosed token | Permission scope; least privilege; revocation; verification | C2: select and verify the least-privilege access action |
| T3 | Restore an immutable snapshot and verify the restored dataset before cutover | Snapshot behavior; namespace isolation; count and access checks | C3: plan recovery without destroying original evidence |

These tasks are document-derived hypotheses. A real study would recruit practitioners representing implementation scale, role experience, customer environment and accessibility needs. Selection should be justified against the actual population, not a convenient fixed panel size.

## SME workshop and survey instrument

Bring the proposed task table and source guides. Ask each participant to describe recent work, difficult decisions, high-consequence failures, missing tasks and scope boundaries. Test whether the three proposed competencies distinguish independent performance from following a checklist.

For each task, ask whether it is in scope; importance (1 peripheral, 2 useful, 3 necessary, 4 critical); frequency (never, less than monthly, monthly, weekly, daily); consequence of error (low/recoverable, material disruption, serious sustained impact); and missing conditions. Include not applicable and don't know. Report distributions, missing responses and segment coverage, with disagreement preserved. These are proposed instrument anchors, not observed survey results or a universal weighting formula.

## Proposed blueprint and rationale

Eight items: four ingestion, two access, two recovery. At least one practical exercise. This small form is for workflow testing. The 50/25/25 item-count allocation is a synthetic design decision reflecting the example's emphasis on ingestion, not a finding from actual practitioners. Practical exercises have four points, so point percentages differ from item percentages.

The bank includes scenario questions and three practical exercises. D04 directly observes a reconciliation and publication decision. The helper's form proposal selects exact reviewed revisions and at least one practical exercise. A real owner must assess whether the methods and sampling support the intended claim.

## Pilot and standard setting

Use the chosen exact form and documented scoring rubric. Record candidate cohort selection, allowed resources, timing, accessibility conditions, scorer calibration, omissions and feedback. The supplied twelve-row CSV is synthetic software-test data, not a sample-size recommendation or actual pilot. Practical scores are simulated recorded scores, not an inter-rater study.

The three synthetic judgment rows use expected item scores for a hypothetical minimally competent candidate. The panel helper produces raw arithmetic only. A real procedure needs an operational candidate definition, justified method and panel, training, recorded rounds and an owner decision on the final passing rule and consequences. No live passing rule is adopted here.
