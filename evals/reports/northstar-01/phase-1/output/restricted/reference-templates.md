## 2. Job-analysis plan and instruments

Study question; target population; represented and missing segments; source inventory/version; SME selection and conflicts; recruitment/consent/data handling; workshop/interview/survey method; synthesis and decision method; limitations; responsible owner.

Workshop prompts:

1. Describe a recent successful instance of this role's work. What was produced and how was success known?
2. What decisions distinguish independent performance from supervised work?
3. Which infrequent errors have serious consequences?
4. What conditions, resources or constraints change the task?
5. What work is outside this credential's claim?
6. Which candidate task statements are missing, ambiguous or redundant?

Task table: ID | action + object | conditions | observable result | enabling knowledge/skills | supporting source/version | proposed/reviewed/studied | inclusion rationale.

Survey draft: respondent role/segment (minimize identifying data); task ID; whether performed/in-scope; importance with explicitly defined anchors; frequency with defined period; consequence of error with anchors; missing task/comment. Include not applicable and don't know rather than coercing a rating. Specify how missingness and disagreements will be reported.

Study report: actual participation and coverage; instruments; source/version; response counts and missingness; findings tied to data; disagreement and limitations; included/excluded tasks with rationale; implications for the blueprint; decisions and supporting evidence. Never replace absent data with simulated respondents in a real report.

## 3. Competency model and blueprint

Competency ID | observable capability | linked tasks | performance/proficiency anchors | evidence | limits.

Domain ID | objectives/competencies | weight basis and percentage | rationale | assessment method | target count | critical conditions/constraints.

Exam specification: intended candidate, methods, length, timing proposal, allowed resources, score model, partial credit, method minimums, enemy groups, exposure/alternate-form considerations, accessibility needs, version, approval. Explain why the chosen methods support the claim. List what the assessment cannot establish.

## 4. Item and practical-task authoring

Item ID/revision; mapped task/competency/domain; source location/version; cognitive demand with reason; intended challenge; prompt; options/key or expected deliverable; rationale; maximum score; draft/review status; author; enemy groups.

Practical task: safe environment and starting state; candidate objective; permitted resources; artifact to submit; observable criteria; full/partial/zero credit anchors; scoring examples for raters; time proposal; cleanup and restrictions. Keep scoring examples and keys restricted.

## 5. Review and decision register

Reviewer ID/role and relevant expertise; target ID/revision; review date; evidence reference; real/synthetic label; accuracy/relevance/scoring/language/accessibility/fairness comments; accept/revise/reject; proposed change; resolution, authority and rationale.

Program decision: ID | decision needed | alternatives/tradeoffs | actual decision and actor | target revision | supporting evidence | date | affected artifacts | follow-up. Preserve dissent and superseded decisions.

## 6. Pilot and standard-setting protocol

Pilot purpose; population/cohort and selection rationale; versioned form; administration/resources; scoring and omissions; data access/retention; feedback instrument; analysis questions; exclusion rules; limitations; actual report location.

Candidate feedback: unclear instructions/items, task realism, timing, technical/accessibility obstacles and optional context. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.

Standard setting: minimally competent performance definition; method rationale; panel selection/training; forms/versions; round instructions; individual judgments; discussion/disagreement; arithmetic summary; policy/consequence review; uncertainty; chosen rounding/rule; actual owner decision. Keep synthetic practice rounds separate from real panel judgments.

## 7. Launch and candidate guide

Release inventory: exact program/blueprint/form versions; study evidence; reviews; owner decision; delivery mapping and import check; scoring verification; accessibility check; support/incident owner; remaining issues; destination receipt/readback.

Public candidate guide: purpose and audience, scope/domains, eligibility, format/timing, permitted resources, registration, accommodations process, result communication, retakes, appeals, renewal, support. Never include live keys, restricted rubrics, reviewer comments or candidate records. Give approved practice material a separate identity.

## 8. Operations and cases

Credential intent: pseudonymous candidate ID, credential/version, eligibility/result evidence, decision ID, stable intent ID, exact provider action, authority, submitted/unknown/observed state, receipt, reconciliation. Read back an unknown outcome before retry.

Case: ID, category (appeal/accommodation/incident/correction), restricted evidence, owner, requested outcome, deadlines, decision/authority, actions/receipts, communication status, retention. Do not overwrite original results or announce a decision before authorization.

## 9. Maintenance and transition

Trigger/source changes; exact old/new versions; affected tasks/competencies/items/forms/guides; semantic impact; coverage after withdrawal; revision/review/study needs; release proposal; treatment of existing credentials; owner decisions; transition communications; historical snapshot; retention/retirement actions.
